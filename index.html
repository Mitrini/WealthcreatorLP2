The problem no one warned you about

At Age 73, the IRS Forces You to Withdraw — Whether You Need the Money or Not.

It’s called an RMD — Required Minimum Distribution. The government decides how much you must pull out of your 401(k) or IRA every year, and you pay income tax on every dollar. No exceptions. No negotiation.

For savers with $250,000 or more, those forced withdrawals can push you into a higher tax bracket, trigger IRMAA Medicare surcharges, and tax up to 85% of your Social Security. Three bills you didn’t know were coming — all at once.

For a large 401(k), forced withdrawals can add up to hundreds of thousands of dollars in taxes over a retirement — and the number grows as the account compounds. The call shows you your own estimate.

The good news: there are legal tax strategies that may reduce that burden by 50% or more. But you have to act before the withdrawals begin.

The 9 Tax Traps That Torment Retirees!

If you've been saving in a 401(k) or IRA for more than 10 years, you may be facing at least 4 of these 9.

1
The Roth Conversion Secret

Everyone says a Roth conversion means a bigger tax bill. Almost no one tells you that, with the right strategy, you may be able to convert and still cut your taxes by 50% or more when you file your return the next year. That’s the part we walk you through.

2
The RMD Ambush

Forced withdrawals at 73 that can push you into a higher bracket than you ever paid while working.

3
The IRMAA Surcharge

Medicare Part B and D premiums that spike when your income crosses certain thresholds — often triggered by a single RMD.

4
The Social Security Tax Trap

Up to 85% of your Social Security benefits become taxable income once your combined income exceeds a certain level.

5
The Widow’s Penalty

When a spouse passes, the survivor files single — and often faces a much higher tax rate on the same income.

6
The Heir’s Tax Burden

Under current law, most non-spouse heirs must empty inherited IRAs within 10 years — and pay taxes on every distribution.

7
The Conversion Window Trap

There’s an optimal window to convert before 73. Every year you wait without a plan is a year of that window closing permanently.

8
The W-2 “no options” myth

You may have been told that a W-2 paycheck means your hands are tied. There may be more options than you think.

9
The one-roof advisor gap

A tax strategist, a CPA, a financial advisor and a tax attorney who actually work together are rare. Learn what to look for, and why it matters.
